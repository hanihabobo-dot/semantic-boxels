# Review of slam_collect/ARGUMENT.md (adversarial reviewer, 2026-11-06)

Status: FINAL (2026-11-06, 15:30). Besides reading and recounting, this review ran the author's probe twice on the laptop through copies (the reproduction, 2.1; the rule S4n, 2.4) and made two read-only paired comparisons (2.4). No TAMPURA planning step was run, nothing on the cluster.

## Verdict

**Survives with changes.** As a document it is careful and checkable. Every file:line it cites into their environment, their planner, their configs and our repo says what the document says it says (208 distinct references opened, none WRONG, none missing; five are used for more than the cited text supports), and every number in its two probe tables (3.5 and 3.6) recomputes exactly from the author's JSON; a rerun of the two rules behind its main recommendation reproduces all 120 stored episodes step for step (section 2.1). Its description of their task (section 2) is the most useful part and holds as written, including three findings that any slam_collect work must face (their filter ignores a failed pick, one particle in collision ends an episode under their reward, and three of the twenty maps, whose goal or a target lies within 0.46 m of an obstacle, were never won under it by any scripted rule). Its case for spending about a week before 2026-12-04 does not survive as written. By its own probe, the best structure it found scores 0.58 to 0.60 under the paper's criterion on seeds 0 to 19 (0.61 to 0.63 on held-out seeds), within about one standard error of the paper's WAO (0.56) and far below TAMPURA's published 0.81: that confirms the no-fit case's objection 3.4.3 rather than answering it. The only criterion on which the probe shows a gain is the world-state test the document adds, and most of that gain comes from our estimator reading fields their filter ignores (the true collision flag, the failed pick), not from the planner. The answer to Hani's question is right in substance (a small fluent set, constant over the episode) but not because of boxels: their observation speaks only of eleven named regions, TAMPURA's own abstraction already has a constant 49 atoms, all free space merges away in the recommended design, and its one remaining spatial ingredient, the `tight` clearance facts, has no measurable effect: this review reran the recommended rule without its clearance clause on the document's 100 paired episodes, 83 took the same actions, and the clause won one world test and lost none (p = 1.0; section 2.4). Nor is the predictive rule measurably better than looking before every commit (S1o), which needs no drift levels at all. The recommended domain also steers by action costs under a planner configuration that the cited lines of our own code describe as barely respecting costs, and with a place effect that asserts the goal literal even when the place is predicted to end with the pose unknown. What survives is the document's own first step, a pilot of arm T on six seeds, which decides whether there is anything to compare; everything after it should be gated on a go or no-go rule stated before the pilot runs.

## 4. Objections, ranked (most verdict-changing first)

**O1. The probe predicts parity with the paper's determinised baselines under the paper's criterion, and a gain only on a criterion we add, mostly from the estimator.** Under their reward every structure that looks, including the recommended one, scores 0.51 to 0.60 on seeds 0 to 19 (0.55 to 0.63 held-out; tables 3.5 and 3.6), against MLO 0.41, WAO 0.56, eps-greedy plus LAO* 0.74 and TAMPURA 0.81. Under the world test the best structure reaches 0.70, but the document's own split (3.6, 9) attributes about 10 of 60 wins to the estimator's uses (i) and (ii) alone, and D-9 (reading the true collision flag) is exactly the asymmetry a reader would credit to the filter, not to Semantic Boxels. If arm T reaches about 0.81 on this code, the result is "our planning layer scores like WAO"; if it does not, the paper must report that TAMPURA's published number does not reproduce (R6), which is a claim about their paper, not ours. Either way the result does not serve the framing the thesis intro committed to, "a space abstraction, demonstrated on TAMP" (#P10, PA:9505-9508, still open with the supervisors), because of O2. What would answer it: the arm T pilot with a go or no-go rule written before it runs. An example: "stop if TAMPURA wins two or more of the tight seeds 5, 6 and 10 under its own reward and all of seeds 0 to 2 (then its bar is near the published 0.81 and the probe's 0.58 to 0.63 cannot reach it); otherwise continue, with decision time at equal return as the headline". The pilot mixes three tight seeds with three ordinary ones, so its average return is not comparable with 0.81 and should not be the criterion. And the SC11 (b) ablation belongs in the main table, not beside it.

**O2. The design does not test boxels, and the one spatial ingredient left has no measured effect.** In design (c) the regions are theirs (given, not perceived), all free space merges into nothing, and the pose belief is a scalar variance level. The `tight` facts are the only place where geometry of free space enters. The document's S3 against S4 tie does not isolate them (that pair also changes the beacon, 2.3), so this review ran S4o without its clearance clause (S4n): 83 of 100 paired episodes take the same actions, and the clause wins one world test and loses none (2.4). The S4o over S2o gain is the predicted end variance of a hop: odometry, not space; and the predictive rule as a whole ties looking before every commit (S1o) within one standard error (2.4). The drift levels are cut at round numbers, against the recipe the document cites (PROPOSAL:235-237). The honest answer to Hani's question is "the fluent set is small because their observation names only eleven regions (TAMPURA's abstraction has 49 atoms for the same reason); boxels have nothing to merge here except free space, which no action names". What would answer it: drop the `tight` facts or show a map where they change an outcome; compare the levels with S1o's rule as a precondition (section 6) before building them; if levels stay, cut them at the precondition flips (the known threshold, and per place the variance at which the 2-sigma spread reaches the nearest obstacle); a one-paragraph answer to Hani in those terms.

**O3. The recommended gate steers by cost under a search the cited code says barely respects cost, and the optimistic place effect can reach the goal without the gate.** SC4 (b) keeps only the start-of-commit gate as a precondition and puts the predicted end level and `tight` into the action cost. The planner is called with `ff-astar2` (pddlstream_planner.py:1130), whose docstring, cited by the document, records that action costs "barely order the plans" (:1157-1162) and that the first plan found is returned (:1171-1173). Weighted A* with weight 2 and the inadmissible FF heuristic gives no bound on the cost of that first plan, so a direct place that ends at L5 can be returned even when its penalty makes it the dearer plan. Because `move_place` asserts `(target_in ?t ?r)` whatever the predicted end level (ARGUMENT.md:370), such a place reaches the goal in the model, and the agent then stops on an 80 % literal: S2o's false stops, the failure mode SC4 was chosen to remove. A worked case (my analysis, in plain f = g + 2h order; the preferred-operator queue that ff-astar2 alternates with may change it, UNVERIFIED): holding the last target, a direct place of cost c_d + P (P the penalty) against a look of cost h_b followed by a place of cost c_b. The search pops the direct place, a goal node with f = c_d + P, before the look's successor, f = h_b + 2 c_b, whenever c_d + P < h_b + 2 c_b, while the look-first plan is the cheaper one whenever h_b + c_b < c_d + P. For any P in between, a band as wide as a whole commit (c_b, 17 to 30 primitives), the dearer plan is returned. The cost encoding itself is sound (static per-grounding costs, ARGUMENT.md:363-365, 382; our M8 domain notes that Fast Downward's costs are static, domain :318-319); the document gives no value or lower bound for the declared penalty (:382). What would answer it, any one of: assert `target_in` only when the predicted end level is below L5 (a conditional effect, or a precondition on the goal-reaching place, as M8 forces order with preconditions, domain :171-174); declare a penalty above the largest h_b + 2 c_b - c_d over the maps (computable from the hop predictor before any run); or pass a cost-optimal search configuration for this small domain (a call argument, no change to pddlstream_planner.py; the same docstring names 'dijkstra' as blind and cost-optimal, :1170-1171). Then check on seeds 100 to 119 that the planner's looks match S4o's. A stricter K threshold for our own `target_in` than their 0.8 is also open to us and would cut false stops under the world test (O9).

**O4. Unnamed relaxations of binding records.** H1's success-scoring clause, H2's domain swap, H3's "small", H4's structure developed on evaluation seeds, BF#37's parking of B9 and its physical arm O, BF#37's final-world test against per-step reads, BF#66's claim effect, and U-1 to U-4 (section 3). None changes a decision of the arm; each changes what may be claimed and should be put to Hani as a named relaxation, as the find_dice work does.

**O5. The effort is short by two to three days (ESTIMATE).** Not costed: G1, G2 and G13 variants for a new arm O (g13_runtime.py:122-145 is find_dice specific; 3.2 mentions only G2); the calibration runs on seeds 100 to 119 that C7 requires before any run on 0 to 19, and their analysis; BF# entries per step; numeric per-pair costs (`hop`, `commit_cost`) passed through PDDLStream's init (supported as far as I know, UNVERIFIED); a slam sbatch for the cluster (tools/cluster holds only find_dice jobs); the review cycles. Arm T's wall time may exceed 9 h for the reason in 2.3. Realistic minimal version: 7 to 9 working days (ESTIMATE), plus the pilot.

**O6. The replica scores our arm on primitives it did not plan, and its random draws are not isolated as written.** Through D-2 the controllers plan from our mean; the replica re-simulates those primitives from its own particles, so its cloud ends offset from the RRT goal by the difference of the two means, which TAMPURA's belief, the one its controllers plan from, never suffers. In ordinary episodes the offset is small: two estimates of the same posterior mean differ by about sqrt(0.0016 k (1/20 + 1/100)) per axis, 4 cm after a 16-primitive hop and 5 cm after 30 (my arithmetic, ESTIMATE, with independent particle sets of 20 and 100). It is large exactly where the estimators part (a failed pick, a re-seed), and there the replica is already wrong. So the "equals" sentence of 3.3 is wrong in principle but probably harmless in practice; the probe's "the estimator does not move their reward" was measured with an acting belief that is their own code with 20 particles, so it is UNVERIFIED for the design's estimator. The random-stream point (section 3) is a real fix. What would answer it: rerun p6 with an estimator of the design's form (N = 100, uses (i) to (iv)) and report the replica's reward; fix the sentence; replay the replica after the episode from the logged actions and observations (U-1), so that scoring cannot touch execution at all.

**O7. The comparison has little power on success.** One arm T run per seed gives 20 pairs (2.4); only decision time will separate the arms. State the minimum detectable difference, or ask Hani for two or three arm T runs per seed on the cluster.

**O8. The context has moved since the document was written, partly in its favour.** The class_uncertain plan stays held under the dev tracks, its phase 1 handed to path 2 as input; its Route hold was released at 14:14 when the wire_sb_into_tampura argument became final, and whether the plan resumes is Hani's call (class_uncertain/STATE.md:30-38; BF#68, PA:9411-9412). The document's probe already gives that track the trivial baselines (never look 0.34, always look 0.56) and an expected accuracy, and its steps 1 to 3 would give a first arm O number in about 3.5 days (its own estimate).

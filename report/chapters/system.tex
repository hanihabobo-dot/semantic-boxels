\chapter{The System Now}
\label{ch:system}

This chapter describes what changed in the system and why. Nearly all of it is
workstream \PW{1}, with the stacking and reach work of \PW{2} and \PW{3}. The
changes alter every scene, every grasp and every observation, so the numbers of
the thesis do not carry over to the system as it is now
(\Cref{sec:numbers-carry}). Findings of \PW{1} carry the labels F1 to F32 in the
audit file; the report names them where it helps.

\section{Grasping and Release Without the Weld}
\label{sec:grasp}

In the thesis the gripper closed for show and a fixed constraint then attached
the object to it, and release teleported the fingers open (\PW{1}). Both are
gone. The fingers now close on the object, advancing in steps of
0.5\,mm under a close force of 60\,N until both finger pads touch it, and the
object is held by friction; the pads have a friction coefficient of 1.2
(\PW{1}, \BF{35}). Release opens the fingers through the motor, and the planner
judges the result from two renders taken twenty simulation steps apart. The one
hard-coded motion that stays is the short vertical lift after a pick or a place,
which the supervisors allowed (\PW{1}).

Running it with a real grasp found problems that the weld had hidden. Four are
worth knowing.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{The first pick of every episode was a phantom} (F1). The fingers
  spawned closed, the first descent ground the fingertips into the object, and the
  grip check passed on which link touched, not on how it touched. The phantom hold
  survived until a place that released nothing. The fingers now start open and the
  aperture is checked (\PW{1}).
  \item \emph{The simulated arm moved far faster than the real one could} (F14).
  Smoothed paths ran up to 4.2 times a joint's velocity limit in one hop, and a
  cargo was thrown off the table. The step count per waypoint pair is now stretched
  until no joint exceeds its datasheet limit. Three verification runs had no lost
  grip; the cost is that simulated motion time roughly doubles (\PW{1}).
  \item \emph{A transit could knock a bystander flat and the episode still reported
  success} (F18). The planner's collision check sampled a fixed eight
  configurations per edge, so a 63.4\,cm move was checked every 9.1\,cm against a
  3.3\,cm cargo, and the path smoother certified its shortcuts with the gripper
  relaxed along the whole edge. Of 55 recorded
  runs, 13 (24\,\%) had a bystander event. The smoother is fixed. The finer check
  was measured and left off by default, and the entry states that the coarse check
  is unsound on long edges (\PW{1}).
  \item \emph{The swept volume of the held cargo was never collision-checked} (F10),
  because of a pose bug that placed the cargo about 10\,cm too high. It is fixed
  (\PW{1}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Perception Without the Oracle}
\label{sec:perception}

The thesis read exact poses and visibility straight from the simulator. Now the
camera's depth and segmentation images are turned into a point cloud per object,
and a box is estimated from each cloud. On the tested seeds every axis of the
estimated box was within 1.3\,mm of the simulator's own box; a render takes about
180\,ms and the detection about 7\,ms (\PW{1}). Sensing is classified from one
rendered image instead of from simulator body numbers: on 20 comparison seeds plus
one, 96.8\,\% of raw ray endpoints agreed and no shadow cell got a different
outcome (\PW{1}). The planner's collision world is the belief, not the simulator.
The pick aims at the estimate after one more look just before the descent; the
horizontal centre error of the estimate was at most 2.3\,mm over 66 detections
(1.0\,mm on average along the worse axis), so the expected loss of pick success was not
visible at spawn (\PW{1}).

Some reads of the simulator remain on purpose: the verifiers that score an
episode and a log that records the true pose next to the believed one (\PW{1}).
The first scope decision of August also accepted the execution servo's live re-aim
and the planner's collision world as reads of the simulator; the pick now aims at
the estimate and the collision world is the belief, both since 19 September
(\PW{1}).

One bug is worth a sentence because it was invisible to every headless test. In
graphical runs the debug overlay was drawn into the camera image, so the system
sensed its own visualization (F19). The overlay is now hidden around every
perception render, and a probe finds no overlay pixel in the observation
(\PW{1}).

\section{Scenes and Reach}
\label{sec:scenes}

The scene presets changed. Hidden targets are now 3 to 4\,cm (F5) and occluders are
narrow and tall, 6\,cm wide and 7.5\,cm tall in the default scene, so that a
two-finger grasp can take them; the
minimum centre-to-centre separation between spawned objects grew from 10 to
15\,cm (\PW{1}). The scalability scenes did not constrain objects to the robot's reach in
the thesis, although the thesis text says positions are reachable; this
is now set, and the reach was measured with the arm's inverse kinematics: a sphere
of radius about 0.823\,m centred 1.6\,cm above the robot's base frame (world height
0.066\,m; the table top is at 0.325\,m), with a reach of 0.765\,m at 4.5\,cm above
the table that shrinks to 0.604\,m at 30\,cm (\PW{3}).
Every seeded scene is different from the thesis's scene of the same seed. A
consequence of the scene change is that the old evaluation corpus is stale: of 300
candidate seeds 192 still build a valid scene, and eight of the 30 seeds of the
thesis sweep no longer do (\PW{1}). The seed re-roll that used to hide such
failures was removed on Hani's instruction: the seed on the command line is
always the seed that ran, or the run did not happen (\PW{1}).

\section{Stacking and Execution Accuracy}
\label{sec:stacking}

The thesis measured stacking success of 100\,\%, 80\,\% and 13.3\,\% at heights
2, 3 and 4 and attributed the collapse to the simulated arm knocking the tower
over, a manipulation failure and not a planning failure, which it neither fixed
nor fully instrumented (\PW{2}). Workstream \PW{2} added a world-integrity monitor.
After every move, pick, place and stack, one render classifies each registered
object as knocked off the table, lost, disturbed (moved more than 15\,mm),
toppled, or off its support (more than 2\,cm from the support's top). An episode
whose goal object has left the table now ends honestly with the exit reason
``knocked off table'' and no blind retry; a third failure of the same stack ends
it with ``stack give-up''; a stack whose symbolic success the end-of-run physics
rejected is labelled a physics mismatch, where it was an unlabelled timeout before;
and a success with integrity events prints that it is not a clean success
(\PW{2}, \PW{1}). The height-4 collapse has a root cause for the first time: in
three runs with fine telemetry the outcomes differed, and in the clearest one the
carried cube, still descending at 0.67 to 0.70\,m/s, reached the top of the tower
before the planned release, so that the cargo and not an arm link hit the tower
(\PW{2}). The supervisor's long-standing position, that the simulated arm knocks the
tower over, is therefore essentially supported, with one refinement: it was the
cargo, not an arm link (\PW{2}). After the other fixes, the height-3 arm scored 18 of
20 seeds on 20 September (\PW{1}).

\section{The Representation and the Planning Model}
\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{Shadow geometry.} Three corrections changed how shadows are built. For
a tall occluder close to the camera, the shadow collapsed or inverted when the
camera direction was taken in three dimensions, which made a target behind it
structurally unfindable; the direction is now horizontal, with a guard against
degenerate boxes (F3). A carve step deleted a whole slab that contained the
target when a 5\,mm sliver touched it; with the fix, 44 of 44 hidden target
centres over 22 buildable seeds lie inside a shadow cell (F21). An occluder seen
only partly got a floating estimate and cast no shadow; before the fix there were
3 floating slices in 66 bodies, after it none (F22) (all \PW{1}). Two limits remain
and are stated as costs the paper must report: a shadow cell misses the side lobes
of an off-axis occluder (a single 5\,cm cube occludes 253\,cm$^3$, its cell
claims 224\,cm$^3$), and 54 to 69\,\% of the volume of every shadow cell is
visible to the camera, so a shadow cell is not simply ``the occluded space'' (F27,
\PW{1}).

\paragraph{The observation model.} The thesis says a blocked shadow yields no
observation and that moving an object cannot create new shadows; the audit file
records that these sentences no longer describe the code (\PW{1}, notes for
\PW{10}). Now one sensing action classifies every shadow cell against the same
render and removes those seen empty; any newly visible body is registered, so a
relocated object can reveal bodies with new shadow cells (F16, F20); a cell the
planner gave up on is parked as ``unresolved'' and not eliminated (F15); and the
belief collapses when an object is localized. One finding changed what can be
won at all: after the scene resize and until 21 August, no holding episode was
winnable, because every sensing ray ended above the 3 to 4\,cm targets (F5,
\PW{1}). Sensing tolerates up to 5\,\% blocked rays, with a
disclosed cost: a target hiding exactly behind those rays is missed (\PW{1}).

\paragraph{A rediscovery cleanup that was unsound.} In the thesis pipeline,
when an object was rediscovered the cleanup removed its old shadow cells
without a render and gave the area to the planner as free space with a
``not here'' fact. That is a write to the belief with nothing observed
behind it. The plan review of 25 and 26 October recorded it as a finding not
previously in the audit (\PW{4}). The remedy, called M7, is that a superseded
cell is removed only if its grid renders empty. Hani decided on 28 October that it
is on by default everywhere, with an A/B test and a re-run of the thesis sweep
after it lands (\BF{15}); the work package that builds it is still marked open in
the plan (\PW{4}).

\paragraph{Model tweaks for TAMPURA's environment.} Three further changes were
made so that the pipeline can run in TAMPURA's world, each declared with what it
was before, what it is after, and what it changes for a claim (\PW{4}). The pick
now needs the object's pose to be known, sensing takes place from a configuration
in which the arm is out of the way, and a new wrist-camera action looks at one
object (M8, \BF{33}); the grasp is fitted to the estimated rotation of the object's
top face (M9, \BF{30}); and a failed touch makes the object's pose unknown again
(M10, \BF{31}). Hani decided on 28 October that M8 to M10 are on by default on our
scenes as well, so that every thesis number produced before is labelled as
produced without them (\BF{34}).

\paragraph{Planner settings.} The pddlstream search is run with a sample ratio of
0.25 instead of the library default of 1; an isolated comparison gave 31 of 40
successes against 30 of 40 for the default, and the setting bounds a busy-wait in
pddlstream that had doubled wall-clock time on some episodes (F7, \PW{1}). One rule is absolute: nothing edits a plan between the
planner and the executor. A rewrite that removed redundant relocations was reverted
on Hani's instruction, ``never do that. this is cheating'' (F30, \PW{1}).

\section{How the System Is Kept Honest}
\label{sec:honest}

The comparison with TAMPURA is held to an explicit contract written down before
any code ran and binding on every change (\PW{4}). In plain words: no welds,
constraints or teleports of task objects or the robot, and no ground truth feeding
planning, perception, belief update or scoring; the planner stays PDDLStream with
Fast Downward as in the main pipeline, with no domain swap, no plan
post-processing, no hard-coded action sequence and no robot motion outside a
planned action; model tweaks must be small, expressible in the thesis's model,
and reported with a before, an after and the effect on a claim; TAMPURA's own
seeds, scene files and success criterion are used, honest failures are counted
and no code is specific to a seed; and anything that cannot meet these is flagged
and aborted, never worked around. Hani's words of 25 October bind the same
spirit: ``Make little changes and just let our setup work on their find\_dice
problem'', ``Every tweak is honestly reported'' (\PW{4}).

Twenty-one automatic checks, the gates, make the contract runnable (\PW{4},
\BF{2}). They scan the code that a campaign imports for welds, teleports and
ground-truth reads, keep a ledger of executed against planned actions, allow scoring
only through one module, confirm that TAMPURA's own files are unchanged (a hash
of its tracked files, and of the installed copy that one arm imports, \BF{18}), and pin the random seeds and the seed-to-scene map.
Four adversarial reviewers read the first two steps of the campaign, the
parameterisation of the main pipeline and the harness that runs it in TAMPURA's
world: none found a cheat,
a ground-truth path into a decision, a plan edit or seed-specific code, and every
major finding was a missing check, a missing rule or a stale record
(\BF{14}). That review also recorded a limit of the held-cargo check: the planner placed the
cargo 6 to 10\,cm below the gripper while it hangs 2 to 3\,cm below it, and the
entry states that the thesis runs therefore certified every planned move while
holding with the cargo 4 to 8\,cm too low (\BF{14}).

Any comparison needs a noise floor. Running the unchanged pipeline twice on the
same 40 seeds of the holding scene gave 31 of 35 and 30 of 35 successes (seeds 4, 6,
12, 106 and 115 build no scene), three outcome flips and different plan counts in 16
of the 35 seeds (\BF{7});
this is the reason later acceptances use a pre-registered distributional test of
plan counts and not a per-seed rule (\BF{13}, \BF{10}).

\section{Do the Thesis Numbers Carry Over?}
\label{sec:numbers-carry}

No. The audit file states that the scene change alters every seeded scene and
therefore every number, and that the thesis's canonical sweep has to be re-run
after the changes (\PW{1}). That re-run, called CB\#113, was started on
20 September 2026 and is not finished: at its re-aggregation on 24 October it
held 491 of 2160 cells, with 362 successes and 43 scene rejections (\PW{1},
\TK{3}). Its first aggregation counted 372 successes because the result
collector credited a rejected cell with the previous cell's success
(\PW{1}, \TK{3}). A new cluster evaluation of the current code, of the thesis
commit and of TAMPURA on our tabletop tasks is planned and has not run
(\TK{6}). Until then, the thesis's numbers describe the thesis system, and the
report's numbers describe the system as it is now, on the scenes and tasks named.

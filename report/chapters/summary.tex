\begin{summary}
Since the thesis \cite{alassiri2026thesis} was handed in on 10 June 2026 the work has
removed its simplifications, put the system into the environment of TAMPURA
\cite{curtis2024partially}, and opened a set of parallel tracks, thirteen paths with a
fourteenth requested at the checkpoint. On 8 November 2026 Hani named the state of that day Checkpoint 1: the
tracks pause, and this report states that state. Eight points sum it up.

\emph{The system no longer cheats in grasping and perception.} The weld that held
every object is gone and the gripper holds by friction; detection reads rendered
images instead of the simulator's exact poses; failures of the physics are
detected and reported as failures (\PW{1}, \PW{2}). Thirty-two field findings were
fixed or recorded on the way (\PW{1}).

\emph{Against TAMPURA in its own environment, ours is ahead; on generated scenes the
lead is not significant.} On the three scenes that TAMPURA ships, the pipeline with four die-slip
fixes solved 120 of 120 episodes over two jobs, against 209 of 300 without them over five
jobs and 40 of 60 for TAMPURA, one of them crashed (\BF{59}). On 40
scenes from TAMPURA's own generator, ours solved 57 of 80 and TAMPURA 46 of 80, four of
its episodes crashed, with pairs 20 to 9 and exact McNemar $p = 0.061$ (with a scene as
the unit 10 to 5, $p = 0.30$) (\BF{63}). Our run
of TAMPURA lands within 1.8 standard errors of its published return: a mean discounted
return of 0.504 against the paper's 0.63 $\pm$ 0.07 (\Cref{sec:published-fd}). In the mirror experiment, TAMPURA's planner on our scenes
solved 6 of 10 where ours solved 9 of 10 (1 crashed), with TAMPURA's execution kinematic
(\TK{5}). \validitynote{failures}

\emph{The success criterion is under review, because the first close look found drops.}
The numbers above count success by each arm's own end test (ours the pipeline's goal check, TAMPURA's its reward); on 15 November Hani decided that it stays so, with ours reported as the stricter criterion (\ID{decisions}{Hani's decisions}). On 7 November Hani watched two runs of
ours in which a tall box was dropped during a carry, and reported on 8 November that both runs still succeeded
(TB-23, \TK{3}). Two causes were found. The carry's jerks swing the box out of the
grip, and a switch that already exists is the candidate cure: in the A/B of 12 November none of 48
episodes lost its grip on the box with it, 20 of 48 without it (4 seeds build no scene), at no
measurable cost per carry. The other cause is
a carry that strikes an object the robot has not seen; it goes to a new path. By rule 18 of 8
November every evaluation must record drops and collisions and print clean success beside
success. The fields are built in the arms of paths 4, 7 and 10, not yet in our own pipeline (TB-22), and no evaluation has run since; the numbers of this report are not re-scored (\Cref{sec:path3}, \Cref{sec:catalogue}).

\emph{Our occlusion model does not model where the die hides on TAMPURA's task.}
The die lies in none of our hiding regions, which begin at the back face of the
object that casts them; we find it only by lifting the cup over it, which we do
because the space behind the cup is unknown. The paper must not claim more
(\BF{65}).

\emph{The classical planner LW1 drives our robot in a 3-D Wumpus world.} It won 18
of 18 episodes with our perception as its observations, none of the 369 percepts
wrong; LW1 alone makes fewer planner calls and shorter runs than its paper's
Table 2 at 5x5 (\TK{4}). A straight-line walk wins 18 and 15 of 100 of LW1's
states at 5x5, a walker that never refuses an unknown cell 34, so the test has a luck
floor of about a third; no published number for these walkers appears in the papers
read for this report (\TK{10}, \Cref{sec:published}). Our own setup has no result there
at the checkpoint and must clear this floor, not zero (\Cref{tab:wumpus-bar}).

\emph{The thesis's ``timeout'' share on stack needs correcting.} Of 29 rows
labelled ``timeout'' in the thesis sweep's semantic stack cells, 25 ended after 8
to 21 seconds, a symbolic success that the end-of-run physics check rejected
(\BF{68}).

\emph{Two other tasks give a first result for the planning layer alone.} Our planning
layer on TAMPURA's own execution functions reaches their reward in 18 of 20 \cuncert\
episodes and 17 of 25 \slamcol\ episodes; neither task exercises our perception or
physical execution (\TK{2}).

\emph{What is open.} Nothing has run on the cluster. The cluster comparison of our
code, the thesis code and TAMPURA is planned, and the amendment that reads our test on
TAMPURA's final world, as a number beside TAMPURA's own reward, is built and checked
but has never run (\BF{70}). The
new paths on the \cuncert\ task, on the single look action and on our setup in
the Wumpus world have built code and run no episode. Hani's decisions wait in one
round (\Cref{ch:open-questions}): the default of the acceleration-limited carry after
Hani's visual check, the headline success criterion, the questions of paths 4 and 7 to 10,
and what to delete. Hani's visual check under rule 15 is on record for four runs, two per
system on path 5's scenes, and for no approved comparison (\Cref{sec:visual-check}). The
wedge of WSL on 7 November has no established cause (\BF{69}).
\end{summary}

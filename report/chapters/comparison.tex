\chapter{The Comparison with TAMPURA}

\section{Why the Comparison Had to Be Redone}
\label{sec:why-redo}

The thesis compared its own task with TAMPURA's on different environments
(\Cref{sec:thesis-tampura}). What the paper needs is our pipeline and TAMPURA on
the same task, the same scenes and the same success rule. Hani put it this way on
25 October 2026: ``run this on TAMPURA's env vs the TAMPURA system on the same
seeds and see who did better, how quickly'', and ``No welds, no oracles, no
hardcoded solutions. The planner stays the same'' (\PW{4}).

An earlier attempt had to be thrown away. The first bridge between our pipeline
and TAMPURA reported 20 of 20, but only because the bridge code chose which cup to
move and where to place it, parked the arm before each sensing action, declared
shadow cells empty without an observation, welded the grasp, and wrote ``at home''
and ``success'' into TAMPURA's own belief. Its planner only ever emitted five
actions in a fixed order. Hani discarded it on 20 August 2026: ``that work was
trash. we should start over'' (\PW{4}). Everything in this chapter comes from the
restart of 25 October, on the honesty contract of \Cref{sec:honest}.

\section{The Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

TAMPURA's \fdice\ task has a Franka Panda with a wrist camera, a table, one die and one
to five upside-down cups; the die is hidden under a cup. The released code ships exactly three scene
files, so TAMPURA's 20 seeds are 20 draws over three layouts, and in all three
the die sits inside a cup of the larger sizes (\PW{4}). TAMPURA's world is
kinematic: its executor moves joints and the carried die by setting poses, takes no
physics step, and a pick cannot fail physically. Once its grasp planning succeeds, the die is
carried by a reset of its pose to the gripper at every waypoint, and in a measured
run the carry home made no simulation step and no motor call; ``their die cannot
slip'' (\BF{35}). Its success test reads, from the
abstract belief, whether the die is held and the arm is at home; holding is set
whenever grasp and motion planning succeed, without a contact check, and being at
home is a joint-angle comparison; the go-home step reports the home pose even when
its motion plan failed, so the belief can read the arm as at home with the arm never
moved. The audit file did not check whether
this path occurs in TAMPURA's 20 seeds (\BF{35}).

Our pipeline runs inside that world through an adapter, with our own perception,
belief, planner and physical execution. Our copy of the world has gravity, a
finger-pad friction of 1.2 and a 50-step settle; in two of the three layouts (15 of
the 20 seeds) the die falls about 4\,cm deeper into its cup before the run starts
(\PW{4}). The camera is fixed at the pose
of TAMPURA's wrist camera at its home configuration, and it observes after every action
without the arm moving, whereas TAMPURA looks only when it chooses to look, from 20 to
30\,cm above an object (\PW{4}). Both arms get 900 seconds per episode;
TAMPURA has its 20-step horizon and our arm has no step limit (\PW{4}, \BF{68}). The complete list
of deviations is in \appref{ch:appendix-deviations}.

\section{The Three Shipped Scenes}
\label{sec:shipped}

TAMPURA as shipped, run on the chair's cluster over seeds 0 to 19, solved 13 of
20 (Wilson interval 0.43 to 0.82); the thesis's laptop run solved 11 of 20 (0.34
to 0.74), which the entry calls no difference at $n = 20$. One cluster episode, seed 16,
crashed inside TAMPURA at the first step and is counted as a failure (\BF{9}).
Which scene a seed loads depends on the order in which the file system lists the
scene files, so the laptop and the cluster load different scenes for the same
seed; a per-seed comparison with the thesis holds for seven seeds only (\BF{8},
\BF{39}).

The die-slip campaign (\Cref{sec:holding-findings}) compared our pipeline with
TAMPURA on the same seeds, three repetitions each. With the four chosen switches
our pipeline held the die in 60 of 60 carries and solved 60 of 60 episodes, and a
second job replicated this; the same pipeline without the switches solved 168 of
240, and TAMPURA 40 of 60 (13, 14 and 13 per repetition; one crash, which the entry
calls censored but which sits inside the 60) (\BF{59}). The entry says that these seeds load only three scenes, that TAMPURA runs
PyBullet's 50 solver iterations against our 200, and that the comparison is
planner against planner, each in its own simulation (\BF{59}). Later entries pair
the 59 valid TAMPURA episodes with ours: both succeed in 40, only ours in 19, only
TAMPURA in none (\BF{63}). These results hold for the three shipped layouts only.

\section{Forty Generated Scenes}
\label{sec:generated}

The shipped seeds are only three layouts, and the paper publishes no seed list.
Hani asked for ``at least 20 seeds vs 20 seeds that work'' and ``no incomparable
results'', and agreed to scenes from TAMPURA's own generator with its code untouched
(``yes, maximally so'') (\BF{63}). The design was declared before any run: 40
scenes (seeds 100 to 139; 7 with one cup, 9 with two, 8 each with three, four
and five), nothing filtered out, two repetitions, TAMPURA's planner first and ours
second on each scene on one node, a validity rule fixed in advance, and Hani

\paragraph{Run 1 and the slow hour.} In run 1 ours won 25 pairs to 6 and the test
gave $p = 0.00088$. The entry first read the slowdown of TAMPURA as busy nodes, and
corrected this on 4 November: it was one hour, from about 21:00 to 22:00, on every
node at once. Our planning calls ran 15 to 24 times slower while execution steps
kept their speed, because planning writes its files on a network volume; all 14 of
TAMPURA's timeouts began between 21:00 and 21:44. Split after the fact by that hour,
the 45 pairs outside it read 31 of 45 for ours against 25 of 45 for TAMPURA with
$p = 0.21$, and the 33 pairs inside it 24 of 33 against 11 of 33 with $p = 0.00098$
(\BF{63}). The entry's rule: run 1 is cited with its slow hour or not at all.

\paragraph{Run 2 decides.} Run 2 repeated the 40 scenes with the changes declared
in advance (each job's files on the node's own disk, and a probe that logs the
volume's latency). Ours solved 57 of 80 again, TAMPURA 46 of 76, the pairs split 18
to 9 and the exact test gave $p = 0.12$; each repetition alone gave 0.42 and 0.27
(\BF{63}). The entry notes that in run 2 the two repetitions of a scene are close
to one draw (TAMPURA's outcome was the same in both repetitions on 38 of 38 scenes),
so the 76 pairs carry about the information of 38 and the per-repetition values are
the fairer reading (\BF{63}). Counting our episodes under TAMPURA's 20-step budget,
which our arm did not have, moves the pairs to 17 against 10 with $p = 0.248$
(\BF{68}). Ours is ahead; the lead is not significant.

The check has three results. First, our runs of TAMPURA reproduce its published
reliability: the success rates of 0.55 to 0.67 across four independent runs
(the thesis run, the cluster run, the die-slip campaign and run 2 of the generated
scenes; run 1 of the generated scenes, 0.46, is left out because of its slow hour)
bracket the 0.63 that the return implies, and the return of 0.530 on generated
scenes is within 1.4 standard errors of the published 0.63. Second, the number of
actions to success is somewhat higher in our runs. Third, TAMPURA's time per
episode in our runs is lower than the product of the published per-step time and
step count, which the thesis had also found for its per-step figure; hardware
differs, and so do scenes. The audit file notes that the steps differ in meaning and
that TAMPURA's per-step time includes 200 samples of its effects function, and drops
the per-step comparison for that reason; it confirms no cause (\PW{4}). For our pipeline
no published counterpart exists. Our own convention for counting its actions,
which gives a return of 0.585 over 80 episodes, is an indicator and not a
pre-registered number (\ID{bf63step}{step-count file}).

\section{Why Our Failures Happen}
\label{sec:why-fail}

Our failures on the generated scenes are one mode. The pipeline fails on 23
counted episodes in both runs, in the same 12 scenes and with no lost die; in run
1, 17 end with ``all shadows searched, target not found'' while the die lay under
a cup that held no registered shadow (\BF{63}). The reason is a property of the
representation. Our hiding regions are the space behind an object as the camera
sees it, never the space under it, because every shadow starts at its caster's back
face. TAMPURA's generator puts the die at a cup's pose, and the three shipped
layouts do the same, so in every \fdice\ scene the die starts in none of our regions
(\BF{65}). We find it anyway, because the plan lifts a cup to look behind it and
the refresh render then shows the die where the cup stood. Every success of ours
on \fdice\ came this way: 57 of 57 on the generated scenes and 653 of 653 in the
die-slip campaign on the shipped scenes, in every arm, and no sensing action found
the die in any of them (\BF{65}). The cup over the die is lifted only because the
space behind it is unknown, and whatever removes that reason loses the die.

Where the 23 failures come from is itemised by cause. In 16 of the 17
runs that ended with all shadows searched, the shadow behind the cup was clipped away by the
window in which the pipeline plans free space; the fix for this (\BF{64}) is
written but not yet in the trunk (\TK{3}), and the numbers of the evaluation stand
for the code they measured. In local reruns with that fix, 6 of the
12 scenes succeed and 6 still fail: one because the shadow was a 2.3\,cm sliver
seen empty, two because the reach gate refuses a shadow centre 0.826\,m and 0.845
to 0.853\,m from the base while the dice lie at 0.761\,m and 0.774\,m, one because
the die lay tilted 71 degrees inside a cup and only 6 pixels were visible, and two
because of loops in the object-fitted grasp and the pose belief on a far cup
(\BF{65}). The failures are therefore a mix of a limit of the representation and of
five planner problems, each logged on the board of path 3 (\TK{3}). The audit file
leaves the choice among a region under the cup, narrow fixes, or a stated
limitation in the paper to Hani (\BF{65}).

\section{TAMPURA on Our Environment}
\label{sec:reverse}

The mirror experiment, run by path 5, puts TAMPURA's planner and its \fdice\ model
unchanged into the scenes that our system builds, with glue code only, a timer
the only change to TAMPURA, and our fixed camera as its eye (\TK{5}). Both arms are
scored by whether the target is held at the end in the arm's own executing world;
TAMPURA's execution there is kinematic and noise-free, ours physical. On the ten
seeds that build, ours solved 10 of 10 (Wilson interval 0.72 to 1.00) and
TAMPURA 6 of 10 (0.31 to 0.83); the pairs split 4 to 0 and the exact test gave
$p = 0.125$ (\TK{5}). TAMPURA's four failures: one place ended in a collision and
left it holding an object, and three were look loops, in which it repeated one look
17 to 20 times until its 20 steps were used, because our fixed camera gives its look
action no new viewpoint (\TK{5}). The median wall time was 16.4\,s for ours against
250.2\,s for TAMPURA (\TK{5}). The thesis named this experiment as the gap in its
comparison; no published number exists for TAMPURA on our scenes. A re-run in
which TAMPURA looks through its own hand camera for its looks (option B) stopped after
five seeds (0, 2, 3, 4 and 5), all successes. Go read Hani's words of 7 November as
making the version with the hand camera for every image, the first as well (option
C), the deliverable. Its batch started at 13:18 and ended at 13:48 under the stop
order for WSL with two seeds done, seed 0 a success and seed 2 a failure; the other
eight seeds wait (\TK{5}).

\section{What the Paper May and May Not Claim}
\label{sec:claims}

The audit file states the claim effect of each result, and the report repeats them
as a short list.
\begin{itemize}
  \item On generated scenes ours is ahead of TAMPURA (57 of 80 against 46 of 76)
  and the lead is not significant ($p = 0.12$); the paper reports the numbers with
  the test and does not call ours better there (\BF{63}). Run 1 is cited with its
  slow hour or not at all.
  \item The shipped-scene result stands, scoped to the three shipped layouts
  (\BF{63}, \BF{59}).
  \item The paper must not credit the occlusion model with modelling where the die
  hides on \fdice\ unless a hiding region under objects is built (\BF{65}).
  \item The comparison is planner against planner, each in its own simulation:
  TAMPURA's world is kinematic and its die cannot slip, ours is physical
  (\BF{59}, \BF{35}).
  \item The thesis's parity sentences (144\,s against 166\,s, 65.6\,\% against the
  at least 63\,\% implied) are to be deleted unless the intervals support them, and
  a worse result is to be written as such (\PW{4}).
\end{itemize}

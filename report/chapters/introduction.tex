\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

This report collects what the project has learned and changed since the thesis
\cite{alassiri2026thesis} was handed in on 10 June 2026, every evaluation run
since then, and the work that is in progress now. It is an internal report for
Hani and the supervisors of the project. It is written to be read without the code: where the
record uses internal names, the report says in plain words what they mean, and
where a number comes from, a tag says where.

The thesis showed that a classical planner can reason about hidden objects when
the space the robot cannot see is cut into named cells, and it measured the
idea in a simulator that was kind to it: perception was an oracle, the grasp was
a rigid link between gripper and object, and the comparison with TAMPURA
\cite{curtis2024partially} was indirect. The months since have removed those
kindnesses one by one, put the system inside TAMPURA's own environment, and
started a set of parallel tracks that test it on other tasks. The report follows
that line. \Cref{ch:thesis-recap} recalls the thesis and the list of missing
pieces the supervisors drew up from it. \Cref{ch:system} describes the system
as it is now. \Cref{ch:comparison} reports the comparison with TAMPURA.
\Cref{ch:evaluations} catalogues every evaluation and sets the numbers against
the numbers the literature publishes for the same tasks. \Cref{ch:findings}
lists the findings and fixes by theme and says which statements of the thesis
they qualify. \Cref{ch:progress} describes the dev tracks, and
\Cref{ch:open-questions} lists what waits for a decision. Two appendices list
the deviations from the published setups (\appref{ch:appendix-deviations}) and the
internal sources behind every tag (\appref{ch:appendix-sources}).

\section{The Record This Report Draws On}
\label{sec:record}

The live record is one audit file, \texttt{PAPER\_AUDIT.txt}. It has one entry
per workstream, numbered \#P1 to \#P10, and one entry per decision, fix,
measurement or abort of workstream \#P4, which runs our pipeline inside TAMPURA's
environment, numbered BF\#1 to BF\#70 at the checkpoint. Since 6 November the
work also runs as parallel dev tracks (\BF{68}), each with a folder of plans, state
files and evidence. The record is long, more than 10\,000 lines. The report states the
time of the files it read where that matters (\Cref{ch:progress}); the tracks paused at
Checkpoint 1 on 8 November, and the state of each is the one of that checkpoint.

\section{Source Tags}
\label{sec:tags}

A tag in the text names the entry a number or finding comes from. \BF{63} is entry
BF\#63; \PW{1} is workstream \#P1; \TK{5} is the dev track of that number. Each
tag links to its row in \appref{ch:appendix-sources}, which gives the entry's
heading, its status as the entry states it, and the evidence folder or file.
Numbers are copied as the entry states them, with the entry's own rounding.
Where the record disagrees with itself, the report says so
(\Cref{sec:disagreements}). A statement about TAMPURA's released code is our own
reading of that code and cites the entry or track document that records the
reading, never the paper.

\section{Reading the Numbers}
\label{sec:reading}

Most results are counts of successful episodes. A count such as 57 of 80 is
accompanied where the record gives it by a 95\,\% Wilson interval
\cite{wilson1927probable}, the range in which the true success rate plausibly
lies; 57 of 80 is 0.71 with an interval from 0.61 to 0.80 (\BF{63}). When two
systems run on the same scenes, the scenes where both succeed or both fail
carry no information about which is better. The scenes where exactly one succeeds
do. The exact McNemar test \cite{mcnemar1947note} asks whether the split of
those scenes is lopsided: 18 against 9 gives a p value of 0.12 (\BF{63}). A p value
above 0.05 means the data do not rule out that the two systems are equal; it
does not show that they are. ``Standard error'' is the usual spread of an average
and is quoted only where a paper quotes it.

\section{Published Numbers and Our Check}
\label{sec:check}

Hani asked that the report include, for every approach and task where we give a
number, a check against the numbers that the literature reports for that approach
and for the other approaches on the same task, each with its citation. The report
does this in \Cref{sec:published-fd} and \Cref{sec:published}: one table per task, with the published numbers read from
the paper itself in this session (each cited, and recorded with the place where
it was checked), our run with its source tag, and a stated check. The check says
whether our run reproduces the published number, by how much it differs, and for
what known reasons. Where the literature gives no number for an approach, the
report says so and does not estimate one.

\section{What This Report Does Not Do}
\label{sec:limits-of-report}

It runs no experiment, edits neither the thesis nor the audit file, and decides
nothing. The dev tracks paused at Checkpoint 1, so the state of a track is the one of
that checkpoint, with later facts given with their time (\Cref{ch:progress}). Several
decisions belong to Hani and are listed in
\Cref{ch:open-questions} with the options and recommendations the tracks
recorded.

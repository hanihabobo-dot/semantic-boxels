\chapter{Findings and Fixes}
\label{ch:findings}

This chapter groups the findings, fixes and decisions of the record by theme. Each
section says what the finding changes for a reader of the thesis. The numbers and
the results of the evaluations themselves are in \Cref{ch:comparison} and
\Cref{ch:evaluations}; the changes to the system are in \Cref{ch:system}.

\section{Holding the Object}
\label{sec:holding-findings}

\paragraph{The die leaves the pads on the move home.} The first measurement of
TAMPURA's setting found that a light box of the die's class, held by both pads
from 7 to 10\,N once the gripper settled, left the pads on the move to the home
configuration in 91 of 96 probe trials (\BF{11}). The first review of the bridge
code recorded this as a blocker that no work package owned (\BF{14}). Hani fixed
the rule for the remedy before any probe: the first remedy that keeps a die-class
body and a cup-class body between the pads at 0.5\,N or more in at least 95\,\%
of the 96 trials is adopted (\BF{15}); the probe applied it to every cup class
(\BF{38}). The probe gave, for the die, 1 of 96
held at the old 3\,mm squeeze, 0 at 2\,mm, 18 at 1\,mm and 93 at 0.5\,mm; the cup
classes were held in 77 to 96 of 96 at every level and the cube and block in 94 to
96 (\BF{38}). Hani watched seed 0 of \fdice\ at both settings (the 3\,mm run lost
the die with ``finger-pad contact incomplete'', the 0.5\,mm run carried it home) and
made 0.5\,mm the default of every pick of every scene and goal (\BF{44}). A check
of the hold detector against the simulator's ground truth found that it agreed in
all six self-test carries, so the loss at 3\,mm is real (\BF{48}). The
acceptance of the new squeeze on our own scenes (holding and stack goals) was not
run; the probe measured the die and the GUI runs the cups (\BF{44}).

\paragraph{Their die cannot slip.} In TAMPURA's own environment the die is carried
home by resetting its pose to the gripper at every waypoint; in a measured run the
carry made no simulation step and no motor call, and the die's offset in the tool
frame stayed constant to 0.001\,mm while it rose 0.38\,m (\BF{35}). Hani's decision
of 29 October was that the comparison is one to one: the planners differ, and
the environment and the success criterion are the same for both arms (\BF{35}).
How to realise this was left open: carrying our die the way TAMPURA's is carried, so
that both are kinematic, or both physical, which would need a change to TAMPURA's
executor (\BF{35}). The decision of 30 October kept our arm physical (\BF{37}, as path 6's
deviation file cites it, \TK{6}), and the comparison stayed planner against
planner (\BF{59}, \BF{63}).

\paragraph{What the die-slip campaign found.} The campaign's diagnosis
(\Cref{sec:slip-campaign} gives its results) is that the die rotates out of a
near-face pinch during the swing home (\BF{59}). The 0.5\,mm hold's force falls to
0\,N once the fingers stop inside the close's tolerance. How well the die is held
depends on the angle between the pads and the die's faces: held in 79 to 88\,\% of
trials at an exact face, 46 to 51\,\% at 1 to 25 degrees off and 80 to 94\,\% on an
edge. The eye's yaw noise of 9.5 degrees cannot certify a face pinch. Each carry
home turns the wrist's last joint by 168 to 242 degrees. A hold of 10\,N needs 200
solver iterations, because PyBullet's default of 50 expels the 5\,g die (\BF{59}).
The slow tail of episodes is a cup loop: the refresh from the overhead eye
overwrites the pose uncertainty that the wrist's close look had measured (0.4 to
5.5\,mm), and the planner plans up to 90 times until the cap (\BF{59}). The four
fixes that survived (\Cref{tab:slip}) are on by default since 2 November (\BF{62}).

\paragraph{Planner fixes found in the four-cup runs.} Watching four-cup scenes
found three planner faults. A move was certified from the approach pose alone, so a
place whose contact pose had no inverse kinematics was accepted and then aborted;
now the contact pose is solved from every approach configuration (\BF{45}). Round
cups, whose registry boxes are squares with an arbitrary fitted yaw, were sized
along the pads and exceeded the span of the gripper at most yaws (a 69.6\,mm box
reads 76\,mm at 5 degrees and 98\,mm at 45 degrees), so no grasp existed; the
span now reads the box's side along the axis nearest the pinch direction (\BF{46}).
A third failure, in which a mis-placed cup is bumped on the move home and the die
is lost, was recorded with two candidate causes not separated (\BF{47}); path 3
holds it on its board (\TK{3}).

\paragraph{What the thesis certified while holding.} The first review of the
bridge code also found a limit of the thesis pipeline. Every pick and place
configuration carries a grasp offset of 6 to 10\,cm below the gripper, while the
real cargo hangs 2 to 3\,cm below it. A planned move after a pick therefore
checked the cargo 6 to 10\,cm below the gripper, and the entry concludes that ``the
thesis runs therefore certified every planned move while holding with the cargo 4
to 8 cm too low'' (\BF{14}). A repair in the planner's model is a change to the main
pipeline with its own comparison; the entry does not schedule it (\BF{14}).

\section{Time}
\label{sec:time-findings}

The baseline recorded on 30 October is one GUI run of seed 10 with four cups: a
success in 7 plans, 154.5\,s of wall time, of which planning took 42.9\,s and
execution 105.8\,s over 31 steps; every sense was preceded by a move home, four
returns costing 18.1\,s, because the model certifies a sense view only for the
current configuration, the home configuration and the retract arc (\BF{52}). Three
versions of the fold of the sense into the approach to the pick each raised planning
time, and two of them also cut execution time (\BF{52}). Hani kept only the search
settings (\BF{57}); the fold returned the same day as a switch for Hani's own test
and is on by default (\BF{52}). The search settings of the planner were the one change that
shortened episodes (\Cref{sec:time-cuts}). Times measured on the cluster before 28
October saved screenshots and are not comparable with times measured after the
change that stopped them (\BF{17}).

\section{Keeping the Comparison Comparable}
\label{sec:comparable}

\paragraph{TAMPURA's tree and robot.} The TAMPURA environments tree (commit
4167c35) does not import as shipped: it star-imports a module that the same commit
deleted. The thesis's laptop run of 11 of 20 was made on a tree with one import fix,
which the comparison now keeps, pins by hash and discloses (\BF{3}). The first measurement of TAMPURA's setting (\BF{4}, \BF{11}) found a
robot whose open gripper position of 0.045 lies beyond its own joint limit of
0.04, finger pads that end 7.2\,mm beyond the grasp target, and a die of about
17\,mm that falls and tilts inside its cup after the physics set-up: 37.7\,mm
deeper in one layout and 39.8\,mm in another, tilted 21 and 49.8 degrees. The die's
mass is 5.2\,g and the cups' 13 to 21\,g (the entry cites Calli et al. for the
masses). The eye is at (0.1786, 0.0488, 0.5883) with a vertical field of view of
49.134 degrees and an image of 640 by 480 pixels, and the arm's reach is a sphere of
radius 0.810\,m with its centre at 0.124\,m height (\BF{4}, \BF{11}).

\paragraph{Seeds and scenes.} A seed's scene depends on the order in which the file
system lists the scene files, so the laptop and the cluster load different scenes
for the same seed (\BF{8}). The campaigns use the cluster's map: seeds 1, 2, 3, 4, 8,
14, 15 and 18 load one layout, seeds 5, 6, 10, 17 and 19 another, and seeds 0, 7, 9,
11, 12, 13 and 16 the third (\BF{8}). A GUI run on the laptop prints the mismatch
and goes on, flagged; headless runs stop; no run is scored from a GUI (\BF{39}).

\paragraph{One success test.} TAMPURA's own test reads, from its abstract belief,
whether the target is held and the arm is at home; the carry is by pose resets
(\BF{35}). Hani decided on 29
October that both arms get one environment and one criterion (\BF{35}), and on 7
November Hani decided for path 6 that our physical world-state test is the standard
success test of both arms, with TAMPURA's own reward beside it (\TK{6}).

\paragraph{Noise and the test for plan counts.} Run to run, the unchanged pipeline
on our holding scene gave 31 of 40 and 30 of 40 successes, three outcome flips and
different plan counts on 16 of 35 seeds (\BF{7}). A per-seed plan-count rule fails
a change that does nothing, about 96\,\% of the time, so the record replaced it by a
permutation test fixed before any run: reference and candidate each run 40 seeds
three times, interleaved, and the test passes when its $p$ value is at least 0.05
(\BF{13}, \BF{14}).

\section{What Makes a Number Trustworthy}
\label{sec:trust}

The comparison runs under automated gates and a ledger of every run
(\Cref{sec:honest}). The first review of the bridge code (\BF{14}) used four
adversarial reviewers with four lenses: cheating, whether the changes stay within the
thesis's formalism of partially observable deterministic planning, the validity of the
comparison, and feasibility. Each returned ``revise'', with 47 findings merged into
37. No reviewer found a cheat, a ground-truth path into a decision, a plan edit or
code specific to one seed; every major finding was a missing check, a missing rule
or a stale record (\BF{14}). Nine fixes of the gates followed. They include a
runtime check that TAMPURA's code and loop are unmodified in both arms (\BF{16},
\BF{18}), a test that replaces every value read inside the write-only region by
noise (passed on seed 0 with a positive control that fails as it must; seed 1 awaits
the cluster) (\BF{19}), controls that score a held die as 1.0, a held cup as 0.0 and
a die with the arm 0.002 rad off the home pose as 0.0 (\BF{20}), one outcome label for
every episode derived from reward, error and gates (\BF{21}), and a check that no
foreign file sits in TAMPURA's trees (\BF{24}). The gates add little time: 0.051\,ms
against 0.069\,ms per collision check (\BF{25}). The review also found the harness's
``green'' to cover less than its deliverable lists, and reopened B2 until the fixes
landed (\BF{14}, \BF{12}).

Later runs found faults that no review had: the first two campaigns of our arm
crashed on every episode, writing a debug file into a read-only folder, because the
smoke run stops before that line (\BF{54}); the die-slip harness double-counted losses in
two places before the evaluation ran, and was fixed (\BF{59}); and the sweep runner banked a
rejected cell's run as the previous cell's result (\TK{3}). Each is disclosed in
the record; the fix of the sweep runner sits on path 3's branch (\TK{3}).

\section{Cluster, Data and Process}
\label{sec:process}

Hani's directive of 26 October is that evaluation runs always run on the chair's
cloud, not on the laptop (\BF{5}). The first cluster runs ran both arms serially on
one exclusive node of the chair's cluster with a fixed hash seed, and the node's
processor is an Intel Xeon Platinum 8160 at 2.10\,GHz (\BF{6}, \BF{9}); the later
campaigns ran on 8 CPUs of a node, shared in the time-cut evaluation (\BF{53},
\BF{63}). The thesis's own TAMPURA run of
2026-05-26 stays a separate reference on other hardware (\BF{5}). Simulation
screenshots are local debugging only and are kept for a week (\BF{17}, \BF{50});
run data are compacted after a day, deleted after two weeks, and the cluster holds
at most 1\,GiB (\BF{49}). Two infrastructure failures cost time or data. The
cluster's size cap counted apparent bytes (2\,769\,MiB for directories that held
690\,MiB) and removed seven snapshots and seven result archives; what those archives
held beyond the laptop's copy of the B1 to B3 results is lost (\BF{55}). The gateway
dropped three full snapshot uploads in one night, so snapshots now hold code only
(\BF{56}). On 31 October Hani set the rule that every session keeps a ledger of what
it generated and deletes its own garbage (\BF{58}).

\section{What Our Occlusion Model Does on TAMPURA's Task}
\label{sec:occlusion-findings}

The one finding that qualifies the representation itself is \BF{65}, reported in
\Cref{sec:why-fail}: on \fdice\ the die starts in none of our hiding regions, which
begin at the back face of the object that casts them, and every success of ours was
found by lifting a cup. A second finding, \BF{64}, is a clip: the shadow caster took the
window in which the pipeline plans free space for the table, so a cup outside that
window cast no shadow and the cup over the die was never lifted. It accounts for 16
of the 17 failures that ended with all shadows searched and the target not found,
which \BF{65} counts, and the die's centre
lies outside the window in 15 of the 40 generated scenes (\BF{64}). Its fix gives
the caster the table that TAMPURA's own place sampler uses; it is on track/fixes
and not yet in the trunk. The local reruns are a regression check: every scene
solved before that was tried (15) stays solved, and the entry says plainly that
the scenes the fix turns into successes are the scenes the bug was found on, so
they do not measure the fix. The measurement is path 6's arm A$'$ on 200 fresh scenes
(\Cref{sec:planned-cluster}), and the numbers of the generated-scene evaluation stand
for the code they measured (\BF{64}).

\section{Ideas Logged for Later}
\label{sec:ideas}

Four ideas are in the record without results. Ranking the shadow fragments by their
probability per cost, with a search by entropy, would let the planner choose where
to look next; the shadow belief holds no probabilities today, and Hani decided to log
the idea and leave it out of this paper (\BF{60}). Merging boxels by any attribute
that the planner reads, including probability mass, as a family of cells of equal
mass, is Hani's idea of 6 November; the entry finds the merge rule in the code to be
about fifteen lines, recommends a paragraph in the paper, and keeps the belief work
for after the deadline; nothing was built (\BF{66}). Deleting the sense action and
keeping only observe (\BF{32}) is now taken up by path 9's single look action
(\Cref{sec:path9}). Drawing the pipeline's debug cameras in TAMPURA's GUI (\BF{27}) is
now path 1's second part (\Cref{sec:path1}).

\section{Where the Record Disagrees with Itself}
\label{sec:disagreements}

The sources the report draws on disagree in a few places. The report states which
figure it uses and does not smooth the rest over.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Entry \BF{5} says the cluster is reached through the RWTH VPN; the later \BF{6}
  says no VPN is needed. The report follows \BF{6}.
  \item \BF{10} counts one new outcome difference in the B3 re-runs; its correction
  in \BF{14} counts two (seeds 10 and 116). The report follows the correction.
  \item The pads reach 7.2\,mm below the grasp target in \BF{4} and \BF{14} and
  11.2\,mm in \BF{26}; the entries do not reconcile them.
  \item \BF{5}, \BF{6} and \BF{14} keep the marker ``open'' although their work is done
  (\BF{6} reports the set-up finished, \BF{25} that all gate fixes landed), and the
  summary lines of the audit list the workstreams \PW{2} and \PW{3} as open although
  \PW{3} and the main parts of \PW{2} landed on 19 September (the last deliverable of
  \PW{2} was answered on 20 September).
  \item \BF{40} gives the submission time of the first campaign as 14:07, \BF{45} as
  13:53.
  \item \BF{44} reads the GUI run that Hani watched with ``squeeze 0'' as a 0.5\,mm run;
  \BF{48} shows that it passed no flag and ran the 3\,mm default, recorded as
  \texttt{grip\_squeeze\_mm} 3.0. The report follows \BF{48}.
  \item \BF{59} counts TAMPURA's 60 episodes on the shipped scenes as 40 successes, the
  one crash included as a censored failure; \BF{63} counts 40 of 59 valid episodes for
  the same scenes. The report gives both where it uses them.
  \item Our mean discounted return on run 2 of the generated scenes is 0.585 over 80
  episodes by the step-equivalence convention, 0.592 over the 76 pairs, 0.600 over 80
  without the observes, and 0.698 over 80 under the one-action harness of today
  (\ID{bf63step}{step-count file}; the 0.592 is in \ID{bf63review}{the review file}).
  The report quotes 0.585 and names the convention.
  \item Hani's visual-check rule (rule 15) is dated about 13:22 in most files (the
  \ID{tracksstate}{tracks' state file}, \BF{68}, and the files of paths 6, 8, 9 and 10) and 13:25, the
  time of Go's relay, in a few (the files of paths 3, 7 and 10). The report gives no
  time for the rule.
  \item Path 9's file first assumed that the inspect sensor of path 8 reads the class
  from the render, and withdrew this when it agreed the interface with path 8
  (\TK{8}, \TK{9}).
\end{itemize}
